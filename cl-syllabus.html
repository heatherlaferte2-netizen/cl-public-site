About This Course

What This Class Is

“This class is about reading things worth reading and writing things worth writing. You will read novels, poems, essays, and speeches — some written centuries ago, some written last year. You will write arguments, analyses, narratives, and things I cannot entirely predict. You will think carefully about language, because language is how humans make meaning — and how meaning sometimes gets unmade. I will work hard in here, and I will ask you to work hard too. That is the deal.”

10th grade ELA at El Dorado High School is aligned to the Arkansas 2023 ELA Standards for grades 9–10. The course addresses all five ELA strands across four thematically organized units built around genuine questions worth pursuing — not questions with predetermined answers, but ones we investigate together through reading, writing, and discussion.

What You Will Do Here

  • Read complex literary and informational texts closely, with genuine attention to how language works
  • Write arguments, explanations, and narratives for real audiences and genuine purposes
  • Participate in substantive academic discussions where your ideas are taken seriously and challenged
  • Maintain a writer's notebook — a daily generative practice that is yours
  • Study grammar and syntax through the lens of craft, including daily sentence diagramming
  • Develop vocabulary acquisition strategies that transfer across subjects and contexts

What You Need

Required Materials

  • Writer's Notebook — a composition book (black and white cover, college-ruled). Bring one by the second day of school.
  • Pens and pencils — annotation is done by hand. No erasable pens for annotation.
  • Anchor texts — announced before each unit. No student will be without a text due to cost — see me privately if access is a concern.
  • Student reference manual — distributed Day 1. Keep it tucked into your notebook's back cover.

Technology Policy

Personal devices — phones, earbuds, laptops — stay in your bag during class. This is a design choice, not a punishment. The most important tools in this class are a book, a pen, and your mind. On rare occasions when technology serves a specific instructional purpose, you will be told in advance. You will never need to charge anything for this class.

Assessment & Grading

How Your Grade Works

Grades in this class reflect what you know and can do — not compliance, neatness, or timeliness alone. Note: If EHS/ESD grading policy specifies different categories or weights, district policy supersedes this.

CategoryWeightWhat's Included
Major Assessments50%Literary analysis essays, research-based arguments, performance tasks, Socratic seminar assessments, on-demand analytical writes. Each graded against specific AR 2023 ELA standards with a rubric.
Writing Process & Portfolio25%Drafts, revisions, process letters, formal notebook check (4× per year). Graded on engagement with process, not only final product.
Daily Work & Notebook25%Quick writes, exit tickets, bi-weekly notebook spot checks, discussion participation, annotation and vocabulary practice.

Revision Policy

All major writing assignments may be revised once within two weeks of return. Revised grade replaces original. Revision requires a revision letter explaining what changed and why.

Late Work Policy

Accepted within one week of due date, no penalty. After one week, maximum 70%. Not accepted after unit ends except in documented circumstances.

Classroom Policies

How This Room Works

Community Norms

We build our community agreements together during the first week of school. Those agreements — not a list of rules I hand you — govern how we treat each other and the work. They are posted in the room and revisited regularly.

Attendance & Make-Up Work

You cannot make up a discussion you weren't in — you can only participate in the next one. If you are absent on a day we read aloud together, you are responsible for reading independently before the next class. See me when you return from any absence.

Academic Integrity

Your writing is yours. The ideas and language come from your thinking — not from AI generation, not from copying, not from pasting without attribution. Using AI to generate your writing produces work that is not yours and from which you learn nothing. The consequence for a suspected submission is revision, not a zero.

Differentiation & Accommodations

If you have an IEP, a 504, or another documented accommodation, share it with me in the first week. I will implement your accommodations fully. If you know something about how your brain works best, tell me.
