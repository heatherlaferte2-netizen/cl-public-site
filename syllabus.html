Welcome

About This Class

This is a reading, writing, and thinking class. We read complex texts closely. We write arguments backed by evidence. We talk about ideas that are worth talking about. And we do all of it in a classroom that makes space for every kind of thinker — because every student in here has something worth saying.

This year we will ask four big questions, one per unit. Every text we read, every essay we write, and every conversation we have is practice for thinking more precisely about these questions. By May you will be a stronger reader, a cleaner writer, and a more confident thinker than you are today. That is the goal. That is what this class is for.

One more thing: this classroom runs on two principles. First: we regulate before we work — that means I check in with where you are before I ask anything of you, every single period. Second: we study how texts work before we argue about what they mean. The how always comes before the what. Those two commitments shape everything we do here.

Course Overview

Four Units · Four Questions · One Year

This course is built around Pre-AP English 2 (College Board), an instructional framework that back-maps from Advanced Placement expectations. It emphasizes close reading, argument analysis, evidence-based writing, and academic conversation. Each unit asks a question worth arguing about.

Language & Power

Pre-AP English 2 · College Board

This course uses the Pre-AP English 2 framework, which means students will complete formal writing tasks at the end of each learning cycle and at the end of each unit. These tasks are scored using a three-part rubric (reading comprehension · analysis · writing). Students will also complete digital learning checkpoints through Pre-AP Classroom. These are designed to look like the kinds of reading and writing students will encounter on standardized assessments and in college coursework.

Materials

What to Bring Every Day

El Dorado High School provides classroom supplies. Students do not need to purchase materials for this class. What students need to bring every day:

  • Composition journal — provided by the school. This is your private writing space. It lives in the classroom. You will write in it every single day. No one reads it without your permission.
  • Academic binder — provided by the school. This is your organized academic notebook. It has dividers and holds your class notes, close reading work, handouts, and writing drafts. It stays in the classroom, organized on the shelf — the school prefers binders stay at school so they don't get lost, so you'll do your studying and review from it during class and in any independent work time.
  • A pencil or pen — you will write every day. Come prepared.
  • Your AR book — keep it in your backpack so it is available when we have reading time. Your reading bookmark — designed and laminated in class — lives in this book.
  • Yourself, regulated. If something is going on before class, that is okay. We check in at the start of every period. You do not have to be fine to come to this class. You just have to come.

Grading

How Your Grade Is Built — 60 / 40

Your grade is split between major (summative) work and daily (formative) work. Grades in this class reflect your ability to read closely, construct arguments, and develop as a writer — not whether you agreed with the teacher or followed all the rules.

Major Grades — Summative

60%
Pre-AP Performance Tasks One per unit (4 per year). Written analytical response to an unseen text. Scored: reading · analysis · writing.
Major
Learning Cycle Assessments (CSA) One per learning cycle (3 per unit). Analytical paragraph(s) in response to a text not studied in class.
Major
Unit Analytical Writing End-of-unit extended essay responding to the unit's essential question using textual evidence.
Major

Daily Grades — Formative

40%
In-Class Work Annotations, exit tickets, close reading tasks, SOAPStone charts, observation charts. Scored for completion and engagement — not correctness.
Daily
Notebook Checks Biweekly informal spot checks + formal end-of-unit check (4 per year). Four sections present, dated entries, engagement visible.
Daily
AR Independent Reading 1 book per quarter. 2 books per quarter. Verified by AR quiz score meeting required point threshold.
Daily
Seminar & Discussion Participation Socratic seminar participation logs. Assessed on preparation, evidence use, and engagement — not verbal confidence alone.
Daily

A note on formative work: Daily work is scored for completion and genuine engagement. If you did the thinking, you get the credit. This is not a trick — it is a recognition that learning happens in the attempt, not just in the correct answer.

Independent Reading

Accelerated Reader (AR) Requirements

Reading independently — outside of class texts — is one of the most reliable ways to grow as a reader and writer. AR is how we track and recognize that reading.

Your teacher will let you know your specific quarterly reading requirement at the start of the year. All students read at minimum one AR book per quarter. Books must meet the AR point threshold and are student-selected — any genre, any reading level that qualifies.

Books must be approved by the teacher if you are unsure whether they qualify. AR quizzes are taken independently through the school's AR system. Your reading bookmark — designed and laminated in class — lives in this book.

Classroom Community

How This Classroom Works

Classroom agreements are made together during the first week of school. The list below will be updated after that conversation. What will not change:

  • We check in before we dive in. Every period begins with Fingers 1–5. Your answer is not graded. It is not judged. It is just information — and it matters.
  • We do the thinking. DOK 3 and 4 — analysis, synthesis, evaluation — is the standard here for everyone. You will be scaffolded toward it, but you will not be asked to do less.
  • We protect the seminar. Academic conversation is a skill and a practice. When we are in Socratic seminar, we use the protocols. We listen. We build on each other's ideas.
  • We own our mistakes. Wrong answers are welcome here. Confusion is welcome. What is not welcome is giving up before trying.
  • Community agreements — developed together during Week 1. Will be posted in the classroom and written in every student's Section 4. Updated after Day 3 (Mon Aug 10)

Policies

What to Know

Absent Work

When you are absent, check the absent work bin for your class period near the door first. Then check your class folder in the crate. Then check the board for any notes. Then ask a classmate. Then, if still unclear, see the teacher. This order matters — you are in charge of getting your work when you miss.

Late Work

Daily (formative) work: accepted up to one week late for partial credit. Major (summative) work: accepted up to the unit's last day for partial credit. After the unit closes, major grades are final. Talk to the teacher before a deadline if you are struggling — not after.

Technology

This is a no-technology classroom during instruction. Phones are away during class — not visible, not in use. Occasional exceptions (Canva projects, Pre-AP Classroom assessments) will be explicitly announced. Outside of those exceptions, the phone is away.

Academic Integrity

Your writing in this class must be your own. Submitting AI-generated text, copying from another student, or plagiarizing a source are violations of academic integrity and will be handled per school policy. If you are unsure whether something counts as your own work — ask before you submit.

Communication

Email is the best way to reach me outside of school. I respond within 24 hours on school days. For urgent matters, contact the front office. Grades and progress are available through the school's grade portal. Conferences are available by appointment during my planning period.

Redos & Revisions

Major writing grades may be revised once per unit. To qualify for a revision, the student must complete a written reflection identifying specific areas for improvement before submitting the revision. Revisions are due before the unit closes. Daily work is not eligible for revision — it is scored for attempt.
